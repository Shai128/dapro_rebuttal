\subsection{Can two-bin DAPRO control the budget without CRC?}
\label{sec:k2bc}

The direct two-bin construction removes an important source of error from the
legacy DAPRO analysis.  There is no longer a high-dimensional oracle policy
followed by a separately fitted Platt or isotonic projection.  At every time,
the deployable low/high lookup values are optimized directly.  It is therefore
reasonable to expect the Phase-I-to-Phase-II cost gap to be smaller.
Nevertheless, two bins alone do not imply expected-budget validity.  They
reduce the complexity of the learned policy; they do not make the empirical
cost of a data-adaptive policy equal to its population cost.

This section separates three statements that should not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the bin table has no additional oracle-to-regression projection
    error;
    \item the fitted table has small empirical-to-population cost error; and
    \item the expected total acquisition cost is bounded by the nominal
    budget.
\end{enumerate}
The first statement holds by construction.  The second is an empirical or
statistical-generalization claim.  The third requires either a quantified
version of the second statement, an independent budget-control sample, or a
pathwise robust constraint.

\subsubsection{Why the legacy projection proof does not establish validity}

The earlier proof compares an estimated coordinate-wise map
$M_t(S(t);\Dcalone)$ with an oracle map $M_t^*(S(t);\Bbar)$ and assumes that
all mixed products of their errors have zero conditional expectation.  Its
conclusion is correct under those assumptions, but those assumptions do most
of the work and are not consequences of median binning.

There are four mismatches with the current soft-prefix hazard-K2 method.
First, the oracle assumption already requires the oracle map to satisfy a
population budget constraint.  Fitting the empirical budget exactly does not
prove that premise.  Second, the required cross-time cancellation is much
stronger than having only two score cells: all bin levels and cutpoints are
learned from the same trajectories, and the score histories and active lengths
are strongly dependent across time.  Third, the current cumulative-logit
correction makes the final continuation at time $t$ a function of the entire
observed bin prefix, not solely a coordinate-wise function of $S(t)$.  Fourth,
the cost depends jointly on the score path, event time, and acquisition
horizon.  Its population expectation must therefore be taken over the full
trajectory, not merely the marginal distribution of one score coordinate.

The legacy consistency discussion also uses, without an independence
condition, a product-of-marginal-$L_1$ bound for products of projection
errors.  That inequality is not valid in general.  Since every error is
bounded by one, consistency can still make each mixed product vanish through
the valid bound
\begin{equation}
    \E\!\left[\prod_{j\in A}|\varepsilon_j|\right]
    \leq \min_{k\in A}\E|\varepsilon_k|,
\end{equation}
but this yields only an asymptotic statement under an explicit consistency
assumption.  It is not a finite-sample theorem obtained from K2.

\subsubsection{A counterexample: even one bin can overrun in expectation}

The following elementary construction shows that no distribution-free
expected-budget theorem follows from using two bins and matching the Phase-I
cost.

\begin{proposition}[Empirical K2 cost matching is insufficient]
\label{prop:k2bc_counterexample}
There exists an i.i.d. two-interaction problem and a time-only policy, which is
a subclass of hazard-K2, whose empirical Phase-I expected cost is at most the
target but whose deployment expected cost exceeds the target, both
conditionally on some policy-fitting samples and after averaging over the
policy-fitting sample.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q=2$, let the active length satisfy
$\mathbb P(L=2)=q\in(0,1)$ and $\mathbb P(L=1)=1-q$, and set the Phase-II
target cost to one.  Consider the following empirical rule.  It always sets
$P(1)=1$.  If at least one of the $n$ policy-fitting trajectories reaches time
two, set $P(2)=0$; otherwise set the unconstrained structural value $P(2)=1$.
For every policy-fitting sample, its empirical expected cost is one.

Let $E$ be the event that all $n$ fitting trajectories have length one.
Then $\mathbb P(E)=(1-q)^n>0$.  Conditional on $E$, the deployment expected
cost is $1+q>1$.  Averaging over the fitting sample gives
\begin{equation}
    \E[c_{\mathrm{deploy}}]
    =1+q(1-q)^n>1.
\end{equation}
The policy does not use a score at all, so it belongs to the one-bin subclass
of every K2 policy class.  Hence restricting a policy to two median bins cannot
by itself rule out the failure.  A positive propensity floor gives the same
construction after increasing the nominal target by the unavoidable floor
cost.
\end{proof}

The mechanism in Proposition~\ref{prop:k2bc_counterexample} is population
transfer, not oracle-to-bin projection.  A Phase-I sample can contain too few
long trajectories or too few particular bin histories, causing their cost to
be underestimated.  The same issue remains when the table is obtained by a
convex DAPRO optimization rather than by the simple rule in the proof.

\subsubsection{Why median-rank symmetry does not repair the problem}

There is a tempting argument specific to K2.  Suppose that $n_t=2r+1$
active Phase-I scores are i.i.d. from a continuous distribution and that the
cutpoint is the order-statistic median.  With the implementation convention
that a score equal to the cutpoint enters the high bin, the fitting high-bin
fraction is $(r+1)/(2r+1)$, whereas an independent score enters the high bin
with marginal probability $1/2$.  More generally, if the cutpoint is the
$k$th order statistic, these two probabilities are
$(n_t-k+1)/n_t$ and $(n_t-k+1)/(n_t+1)$, respectively.  Thus, for a
\emph{fixed} monotone two-level table learned independently of these scores,
the empirical high-bin occupancy has a small conservative rank correction.

This observation is not a budget theorem for DAPRO.  The two table values
are optimized from the same score order statistics, target coefficients, and
active lengths.  Rank symmetry controls a bin count; it does not make the
adaptively selected cost independent of the cutpoint.  The following
one-decision construction makes the distinction explicit.

\begin{proposition}[Median symmetry is insufficient]
\label{prop:k2bc_median_counterexample}
Let $n=2r+1$, $S\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, and let $c$ be the sample
median.  Fix $0\leq\eps<b$ and
\begin{equation}
    w_H:=\frac{r+1}{2r+1},
    \qquad b\leq \eps+w_H(1-\eps).
\end{equation}
There is a data-dependent monotone K2 table with probabilities at least
$\eps$ whose empirical cost equals $b$ for every fitting sample but whose
marginal fresh-sample expected cost is strictly larger than $b$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $c$, let $R(c)=\mathbb P(S_{\rm new}\geq c)=1-c$.  If
$R(c)\leq w_H$, set $p_L=p_H=b$.  Otherwise set
\begin{equation}
    p_L=\eps,
    \qquad
    p_H=\eps+\frac{b-\eps}{w_H}.
\end{equation}
The table is monotone and lies in $[\eps,1]$.  Its empirical cost is $b$ in
both branches because the empirical high-bin fraction is exactly $w_H$.
In the second branch, however, its fresh-sample conditional cost is
\begin{equation}
    \eps+R(c)\frac{b-\eps}{w_H}>b.
\end{equation}
The event $R(c)>w_H$ has positive probability under the beta law of the
sample median.  Averaging over $c$ therefore gives a strict marginal budget
violation.
\end{proof}

This is not merely an adversarial lookup-table phenomenon.  The exact
one-step DAPRO optimizer itself provides a counterexample.

\begin{proposition}[The median-K2 DAPRO KKT policy can overrun]
\label{prop:k2bc_kkt_counterexample}
Let $S_1,\ldots,S_5\stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}
\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, let $c=S_{(3)}$, and put
$B_i=\mathbb{I}\{S_i\geq c\}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 a(S)=\mathbb{I}\{S>1/5\},\qquad
 A_k=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^5a(S_i)\mathbb{I}\{B_i=k\},qquad
 (D_0,D_1)=(2/5,3/5).
\end{equation}
For $0<b<3/5$, fit the exact two-cell DAPRO policy
\begin{equation}
 \min_{0\leq p_0\leq p_1\leq1}
 \left\{\frac{A_0}{p_0}+\frac{A_1}{p_1}\right\}
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad D_0p_0+D_1p_1\leq b,
 \label{eq:k2bc_exact_kkt_problem}
\end{equation}
using the uniform budget-filling solution when both target masses vanish.
Its empirical cost equals $b$ for every fitting sample, but
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\E[c_{\rm new}]}{b}
 =\frac{1921+1280r_\star}{3125}
 =1.0080132589\ldots>1,
 \qquad
 r_\star:=\frac{13/\sqrt2+12}{5(2/\sqrt2+3)}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_exact_kkt_ratio}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $J=\sum_i\mathbb{I}\{S_i\leq1/5\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 3125\,\mathbb P(J=j)=(1024,1280,640,160,20,1)_j.
\end{equation}
For $j=0,ldots,5$, the target counts in the low and high median cells are
\begin{equation}
 (m_0,m_1)={(2,3),(1,3),(0,3),(0,2),(0,1),(0,0)\},
 \label{eq:k2bc_kkt_counts}
\end{equation}
and elementary order-statistic calculations give
\begin{equation}
 \E[c\mid J=j]
 =\left(\frac35,\frac{13}{25},\frac25,
         \frac3{20},\frac3{25},\frac1{10}\right)_j.
 \label{eq:k2bc_kkt_medians}
\end{equation}
For nonzero cell risks $r_k=A_k/D_k$, the KKT equations for
\eqref{eq:k2bc_exact_kkt_problem} give
\begin{equation}
 p_k=\frac{b\sqrt{r_k}}{D_0\sqrt{r_0}+D_1\sqrt{r_1}};
 \label{eq:k2bc_kkt_solution}
\end{equation}
if $r_0=0<r_1$, they give $p_0=0,p_1=5b/3$.  Conditional on the fitting
median, a fresh score has cost $cp_0+(1-c)p_1$.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:k2bc_kkt_counts}--\eqref{eq:k2bc_kkt_solution} and averaging over
the conditional median laws gives the six fresh-to-fit cost ratios
\begin{equation}
 (1,r_\star,1,17/12,22/15,1)_j.
\end{equation}
Weighting them by the binomial probabilities proves
\eqref{eq:k2bc_exact_kkt_ratio}.  The strict inequality is exact:
$r_\star>301/320$ is equivalent after cross multiplication to
$1/\sqrt2>27/46$.
\end{proof}

The strict gap persists under every sufficiently small positive floor and
global regularization by continuity.  It also persists at the exact
production values.  Replace the two cell risks in case $j$ by
\begin{equation}
 r_0(j;\delta)=\frac{m_0(j)+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)},
 \qquad
 r_1(j;\delta)=\frac{m_1(j)+3\delta}{3(1+\delta)},
\end{equation}
use~\eqref{eq:k2bc_kkt_solution}, and apply
\begin{equation}
 \phi_\gamma(p)=\eps+(1-\eps)
 \sigma\{\operatorname{logit}(p)+\gamma\},
 \qquad
 \frac25\phi_\gamma(p_0)+\frac35\phi_\gamma(p_1)=b.
\end{equation}
Exact enumeration of the same six cases at
$\delta=0.001$, $\eps=0.005$, and $b=0.2$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \widehat c_{\rm fit}=0.2
    \quad\hbox{for every sample},
    \qquad
    \E[c_{\rm new}]=0.2012423238>0.2.
    \label{eq:k2bc_exact_kkt_counterexample}
\end{equation}
Hence no theorem whose premises are only i.i.d. sampling, a median split,
monotonicity, positivity, and exact empirical DAPRO feasibility can conclude
finite-sample expected-budget validity.

When the active count is even, the current NumPy median is a linear
interpolation between two order statistics, so even the clean $1/2$ fresh-bin
identity above no longer holds exactly.  Score atoms and deterministic tie
handling can make cell occupancy still more uneven.  These details strengthen,
rather than weaken, Proposition~\ref{prop:k2bc_median_counterexample}.

\subsubsection{Why the scalar exchangeability proof cannot be reused raw}

The single-parameter risk-control proof in the earlier appendix is sound for
a different architecture.  Conditional on an independently learned policy
shape, suppose that $K_i(\lambda)$ is the \emph{same} fixed, bounded, nested
loss family for every exchangeable row.  A selector of the form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\lambda=\inf\left\{\lambda:
 \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_i(\lambda)+K_{\max}}{n+1}\leq a\right\}
 \label{eq:k2bc_scalar_crc}
\end{equation}
can be compared with the symmetric selector obtained after adding the fresh
row.  Exchangeability then bounds the expected fresh loss.  This is precisely
the conformal-risk-control argument.

In raw direct-bin DAPRO, however, both the $\maxl$ cutpoints and the $2\maxl$
table values are learned on the same rows used in the constraint.  If the
shape $g_{\Dcalone}$ is kept fixed when the fresh row is appended, that row is
the unique observation excluded from shape fitting and the augmented selector
is not symmetric.  If one refits the symmetric shape
$g_{\Dcalone\cup\{j\}}$, the monotone comparison underlying
\eqref{eq:k2bc_scalar_crc} fails because the two selectors traverse different
families.  The maximum-loss correction does not repair this mismatch.
Thus the scalar proof becomes applicable only after learning the K2 shape on
independent data and calibrating one scalar on a separate sample---which is a
form of CRC or sample splitting, not a proof for raw same-sample K2.

\subsubsection{A positive, but asymptotic, theorem for unchanged K2}

There is a clean statement that does not use an oracle map or a numerical
projection-error assumption.  For fixed finite $M$ and $K=2$, let
$\Pi_{M,2}$ contain all monotone threshold/table policies and define the
bounded full-trajectory cost
\begin{equation}
 C_\pi(Z)=\sum_{t\leq L(Z)}\prod_{s\leq t}
 p_{s,b_s(Z)}.
\end{equation}
This finite-dimensional VC-subgraph class is Glivenko--Cantelli.  Therefore,
if an arbitrary measurable empirical optimizer $\widehat\pi_n$ satisfies
$\mathbb P_nC_{\widehat\pi_n}\leq b_n$ and $b_n\to b$ in $L_1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\E\bigl[\mathbb P
 C_{\widehat\pi_n}\bigr]\leq b.
 \label{eq:k2bc_gc_limit}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb PC_{\widehat\pi_n}
 \leq \mathbb P_nC_{\widehat\pi_n}
 +\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{M,2}}|\mathbb PC_\pi-\mathbb P_nC_\pi|,
\end{equation}
and the final term converges to zero in $L_1$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:k2bc_gc_limit} is the strongest assumption-light theorem
for the unchanged raw method.  It is asymptotic with fixed $M$; it does not
give the finite-sample inequality $\E C\leq b$.  Generic complexity reserves
for a sequential class with roughly $3M$ learned quantities are vacuous when
$M=200$ and $n\in\{50,100,200\}$.  K2 makes generalization more plausible,
but it cannot turn this limiting theorem into an exact finite-sample one.

\subsubsection{What can be proved from a projection reserve}

Let $\widehat c_f$ be the corrected expected cost of the frozen policy on the
policy-fitting fold, and let
\begin{equation}
    c(P):=\E_{W\sim\mathcal P}
       \left[\sum_{t=1}^{L(W)}\rho_P(W,t)\right]
\end{equation}
be its deployment-population expected cost, where $W$ denotes the complete
latent trajectory.  Suppose the optimizer enforces
\begin{equation}
    \widehat c_f\leq\bar B_2-\eta.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
    \E_{\Dcalone}[c(P_{\Dcalone})]
    \leq\bar B_2
\end{equation}
follows if one separately proves or assumes
\begin{equation}
    \E_{\Dcalone}
      [c(P_{\Dcalone})-\widehat c_f]
    \leq\eta.
    \label{eq:k2bc_expected_transfer_assumption}
\end{equation}
This is a valid expected-budget theorem, but
\eqref{eq:k2bc_expected_transfer_assumption} is its substantive premise.
K2 makes the premise more plausible than a high-capacity projection; it does
not prove it.

A standard learning-theoretic version makes the missing quantity explicit.
Let $\mathcal C_2$ be the class of full-trajectory cost functions induced by
all admissible hazard-K2 tables and let
\begin{equation}
    \mathfrak G_{n_f}(\mathcal C_2)
    :=\E\!\left[
      \sup_{c\in\mathcal C_2}
      \{\mathbb Pc-\mathbb P_{n_f}c\}
    \right]
\end{equation}
be its expected one-sided generalization gap.  Any empirical optimizer obeys
\begin{equation}
    \E[c(P_{\Dcalone})]
    \leq \E[\widehat c_f]
       +\mathfrak G_{n_f}(\mathcal C_2).
    \label{eq:k2bc_uniform_gap}
\end{equation}
Consequently, reserving a certified upper bound on
$\mathfrak G_{n_f}(\mathcal C_2)$ would give expected-budget control without
CRC.  The difficulty is quantitative.  K2 still contains approximately two
continuation levels and one data-dependent cutpoint at each of up to
$\maxl=200$ times.  A generic uniform-convergence bound therefore scales with
the complexity of the whole sequential table, not with the number two alone,
and is typically vacuous for $n_f\in\{50,100,200\}$.  The fact that the
optimizer often pools the two bins in observed experiments can improve
empirical stability, but it does not reduce the worst-case class used in a
distribution-free proof.

Thus the existing one-turn reserve is properly described as an
assumption-based controller.  Its empirical success in mean can support a
sensitivity analysis, but not a finite-sample theorem unless
\eqref{eq:k2bc_expected_transfer_assumption} is stated explicitly.

\subsubsection{A CRC-free, distribution-free K2 guarantee}
\label{sec:k2bc_worst_path}

K2 does permit a different rigorous controller.  It exploits score
monotonicity rather than claiming that projection error is negligible.  Let
$p_{t,0}\leq p_{t,1}$ be the frozen low/high raw continuation table.  The
all-high score path dominates every possible bin path.  Therefore it is enough
to control that single worst path.

We state the result in a form that includes the cumulative-logit correction.
For a raw cumulative path $r=(r(1),\ldots,r(\maxl))$, define
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_\gamma(r)(t)
    :=\eps_\pi+(1-\eps_\pi)
      \min_{u\leq t}
      \sigma\!\left(\operatorname{logit}\{r(u)\}+\gamma\right).
    \label{eq:k2bc_isotone_projection}
\end{equation}
This map is coordinate-wise nondecreasing: if $r(t)\leq r'(t)$ for every
$t$, then $\Psi_\gamma(r)(t)\leq\Psi_\gamma(r')(t)$ for every $t$.

Before any response is generated, the first-prefix hazard and hence the first
bin $b_i(1)$ are known for every deployment prompt.  Define the worst future
raw path conditional on this known first bin by
\begin{equation}
    r_i^+(t)
    :=p_{1,b_i(1)}\prod_{s=2}^{t}p_{s,1},
    \qquad t=1,\ldots,Q_i,
    \label{eq:k2bc_worst_raw_path}
\end{equation}
with the empty product equal to one.  If one does not wish to use the known
first score, replace $p_{1,b_i(1)}$ by $p_{1,1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Worst-path K2 expected-budget validity]
\label{thm:k2bc_worst_path}
Let $B_1=\sum_{i\in\calIone}L_i$ be the realized Phase-I cost and let
$B_{\mathrm{rem}}=B-B_1$.  Suppose the K2 table is score-monotone,
$p_{t,0}\leq p_{t,1}$, the deployment horizons $Q_i$ and first bins $b_i(1)$
are known before acquisition, and a common shift $\widehat\gamma$ is chosen so
that
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i\in\calItwo}\sum_{t=1}^{Q_i}
      \Psi_{\widehat\gamma}(r_i^+)(t)
    \leq B_{\mathrm{rem}}.
    \label{eq:k2bc_worst_path_constraint}
\end{equation}
Then, for every collection of future score paths and event times,
\begin{equation}
    \E_R\!\left[
       \sum_{i\in\calI}\Ttilde_i
       \middle|
       \Dcalone,\{X_i\}_{i\in\calItwo},
       \text{complete latent trajectories}
    \right]
    \leq B.
    \label{eq:k2bc_conditional_budget}
\end{equation}
In particular, the same inequality holds after averaging over trajectories,
the calibration split, and acquisition randomness.  No CRC fold, i.i.d.
generalization argument, correct hazard model, or projection-accuracy
assumption is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a deployment row and its realized bin sequence $b_i(1),b_i(2),\ldots$.
By score monotonicity,
\begin{equation}
    p_{1,b_i(1)}\prod_{s=2}^{t}p_{s,b_i(s)}
    \leq p_{1,b_i(1)}\prod_{s=2}^{t}p_{s,1}
    =r_i^+(t)
\end{equation}
at every time.  The map $\Psi_{\widehat\gamma}$ is isotone, so the deployed
cumulative reach $\rho_i(t)$ is at most
$\Psi_{\widehat\gamma}(r_i^+)(t)$.  Moreover,
$L_i\leq Q_i$.  Conditional on the complete trajectory, the expected number
of acquired interactions is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \E_R[\Ttilde_i]
    =\sum_{t=1}^{L_i}\rho_i(t)
    \leq\sum_{t=1}^{Q_i}
      \Psi_{\widehat\gamma}(r_i^+)(t).
\end{equation}
Summing over Phase II and applying
\eqref{eq:k2bc_worst_path_constraint} gives expected Phase-II cost at most
$B-B_1$.  The Phase-I cost is exactly $B_1$, proving
\eqref{eq:k2bc_conditional_budget}.
\end{proof}

The scalar $\widehat\gamma$ can be found by the same monotone bisection already
used for the cumulative-logit correction; only its cost oracle changes.  The
current implementation evaluates the correction on realized policy-fitting
paths.  The worst-path controller evaluates the left side of
\eqref{eq:k2bc_worst_path_constraint} instead.  Feasibility requires
\begin{equation}
    \eps_\pi\sum_{i\in\calItwo}Q_i\leq B-B_1,
\end{equation}
which is the unavoidable cost of the positivity floor.

The same guarantee may be imposed directly inside the raw log-probability
optimization.  If $y_{t,1}=\log p_{t,1}$, the all-high constraint without the
logit post-processing is
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i\in\calItwo}\sum_{t=1}^{Q_i}
      \exp\!\left\{y_{1,b_i(1)}+
                   \sum_{s=2}^{t}y_{s,1}\right\}
    \leq B-B_1.
    \label{eq:k2bc_convex_worst_path}
\end{equation}
This is a convex exponential sublevel constraint.  Hence worst-path validity
does not destroy the convexity of the soft-prefix Generalized-DAPRO fit.

\subsubsection{A less conservative exact controller: the predictable budget
compensator}
\label{sec:k2bc_online_compensator}

The all-high constraint is the strongest clean option when the deployment
policy must be frozen row by row.  It need not be the best option when the
evaluator processes the active conversations as a cohort.  At every turn, the
set of active rows and their current K2 probabilities have already been
observed.  We can therefore control the \emph{predictable compensator} of the
token count online instead of upper-bounding every unobserved future path.

Fit the soft-prefix hazard-K2 table on all $N_1$ Phase-I rows, without using a
CRC fold.  From Phase I, freeze a strictly positive time-spend profile
$v_1,\ldots,v_{\maxl}$.  One convenient implementation uses the raw fitted
expected spend at every time and adds an arbitrarily small positive value at
otherwise empty times.  Let $R_1=B_{\rm rem}$ be the Phase-II credit.  Just
before the acquisition decisions at time $t$, let $\mathcal H_t$ be the
currently active rows and let $q_{it}\in(0,1]$ be row $i$'s raw K2
continuation, computed from its currently observed prefix.  Define the
tranche
\begin{equation}
    d_t=R_t\frac{v_t}{\sum_{s=t}^{\maxl}v_s}.
    \label{eq:k2bc_tranche}
\end{equation}
Choose a common scalar $\gamma_t$ by bisection and set
\begin{equation}
 p_{it}=\sigma\!\left\{\operatorname{logit}(q_{it})+\gamma_t\right\},
 \qquad i\in\mathcal H_t,
 \label{eq:k2bc_online_logit}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
 c_t:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal H_t}p_{it}
 \leq \min\{d_t,|\mathcal H_t|\}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_online_spend}
\end{equation}
When $d_t\geq|\mathcal H_t|$, set all active probabilities to one.  Update
$R_{t+1}=R_t-c_t$; thus unused credit is carried to later turns.  At the final
possible time, the remaining credit is offered to the remaining active rows.
The score, median cutpoints, K2 table, and task-specific DAPRO objective are
unchanged.  The only addition is the one-dimensional causal intercept in
\eqref{eq:k2bc_online_logit}.

\begin{theorem}[CRC-free predictable-compensator budget validity]
\label{thm:k2bc_online_budget}
Let $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ contain the policy fit, all prefixes, active sets, and
acquisition coins observed before the decisions at time $t$.  Suppose
$\mathcal H_t$, $q_{it}$, $v_t$, and $R_t$ are
$\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable, and conditional on $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ the
active acquisition coins are independent Bernoulli variables with means
$p_{it}$ from~\eqref{eq:k2bc_online_logit}.  If the ledger update satisfies
\eqref{eq:k2bc_online_spend}, then
\begin{equation}
 \E_R[C_{\rm II}\mid\Dcalone,\text{complete latent trajectories}]
 \leq B_{\rm rem}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_online_expected_budget}
\end{equation}
Consequently, expected total cost is at most $B$.  Moreover, for every
$\delta\in(0,1)$, with $x=\log(1/\delta)$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_R\!\left{
 C_{\rm II}>B_{\rm rem}+\sqrt{2B_{\rm rem}x}+\frac{x}{3}
 \middle|\Dcalone,\text{latent trajectories}\right}
 \leq\delta.
 \label{eq:k2bc_freedman}
\end{equation}
No projection-error, score-calibration, i.i.d.-transfer, or CRC assumption is
used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $Z_{it}$ be the acquisition coin for an active row and let
$X_{it}=\mathbb{I}\{i\in\mathcal H_t\}Z_{it}$.  Predictability gives
\begin{equation}
 \E[X_{it}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\mathbb{I}\{i\in\mathcal H_t\}p_{it}.
\end{equation}
The recursion in~\eqref{eq:k2bc_tranche}--\eqref{eq:k2bc_online_spend}
never spends more credit than remains, so on every realized history
$\sum_tc_t\leq B_{\rm rem}$.  The tower property now yields
\begin{equation}
 \E[C_{\rm II}]
 =\E\!\left[\sum_t\sum_{i\in\mathcal H_t}Z_{it}\right]
 =\E\!\left[\sum_tc_t\right]\leq B_{\rm rem}.
\end{equation}
For the second statement, order the offered Bernoulli decisions in any
predictable order.  Their centered differences are bounded above by one and
their predictable quadratic variation is no larger than
$\sum_tc_t\leq B_{\rm rem}$.  Freedman's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:k2bc_freedman}.
\end{proof}

The online theorem is stronger than the desired marginal statement: it
conditions on the complete benchmark trajectories.  It also uses information
that the static theorem deliberately discards.  Rows that stop or reveal an
event release credit to the rows still active, rather than forcing every row
to reserve budget for an all-high, full-horizon future.

\paragraph{HT validity and variance under the shared ledger.}
The common ledger couples the rows, but it does not bias a fixed-target HT
estimate.  Let $R_i$ indicate that row $i$'s required endpoint is reached and
let $\Pi_i$ be the product of the actual predictable probabilities recorded
along that endpoint path.  Provided every offered probability is strictly
positive,
\begin{equation}
 W_i:=\frac{R_i}{\Pi_i},
 \qquad
 \E_R[W_i\mid\text{latent trajectories}]=1.
 \label{eq:k2bc_adaptive_ht}
\end{equation}
Repeated tower conditioning proves~\eqref{eq:k2bc_adaptive_ht}.  Conditional
independence of the same-round coins also gives
$\E_R[W_iW_j]=1$ for $i\ne j$: the rowwise likelihood-ratio martingales have
zero cross-variation even though later probabilities depend on the joint past.
Thus rowwise HT contributions remain uncorrelated.

The familiar retrospective formula $A_i(1/\Pi_i-1)$ treats $\Pi_i$ as fixed
and is not directly estimable from one adaptive-ledger run.  The correct
observed-only design-unbiased acquisition-variance estimator is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V_{\rm acq}
 =\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_i A_iR_i
   \frac{1-\Pi_i}{\Pi_i^2}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_online_variance}
\end{equation}
Indeed, each summand is $A_i(W_i^2-W_i)$, whose expectation is the
corresponding row variance, and the cross-row covariances vanish.

For LPB construction, every fixed candidate's weighted miscoverage estimate
remains unbiased by the same argument.  The finite-sample LPB theorem in the
current manuscript, however, factors a product MGF using mutual independence
of rowwise censoring.  That proof does not apply verbatim to a shared ledger.
The following replacement uses the weaker cross-moment identity above.  Its
dependence on $\delta$ is polynomial rather than logarithmic, but it gives a
complete finite-sample statement without falsely assuming independent rows.

For a fixed candidate $\tau$, write
\begin{equation}
 A_{\tau i}:=\mathbb{I}\{T_i<\fhat_\tau(X_i)\},\qquad
 Y_{\tau i}:=A_{\tau i}\frac{R_{\tau i}}{\Pi_{\tau i}},
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_ht_row}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\tau i}$ indicates that the information required to resolve
$A_{\tau i}$ was acquired and $\Pi_{\tau i}$ is its logged cumulative
propensity.  On a fully observed Phase-I row, set $Y_{\tau i}=A_{\tau i}$.

\begin{theorem}[LPB coverage under a shared predictable ledger]
\label{thm:k2bc_shared_lpb}
Condition on $\Dtrain$ and suppose the full calibration rows and a test row
are i.i.d.  Assume $\fhat_\tau$ is nondecreasing and continuous in $\tau$,
the shared-ledger policy is predictable and strictly positive, and, for all
distinct Phase-II rows $i\ne j$ and every complete collection of latent
trajectories,
\begin{equation}
 \E_R\!\left[\frac{R_{\tau i}}{\Pi_{\tau i}}\right]=1,
 \qquad
 \E_R\!\left[
   \frac{R_{\tau i}}{\Pi_{\tau i}}
   \frac{R_{\tau j}}{\Pi_{\tau j}}
 \right]=1.
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_cross_moment}
\end{equation}
Suppose also that a constant $\bar v\geq1$, which may depend on $\Dtrain$
but not on the Phase-I sample, satisfies almost surely
\begin{equation}
 \E\!\left[Y_{\tau i}^{,2}\mid\Dcalone,\Dtrain\right]
 \leq\bar v
 \quad\text{for every Phase-II row $i$ and candidate $\tau$.}
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_second_moment}
\end{equation}
Let $N=N_1+N_2$ and let $\widehat\tau$ be the same strict-initial-prefix
selector used by the main LPB algorithm, based on
$N^{-1}\sum_iY_{\tau i}\leq\alpha$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\rm ledger}
 :=\sqrt{\frac{(1-\delta)\bar v}{\delta N}}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_gap}
\end{equation}
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over calibration trajectories and
acquisition randomness,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P\!\left[
   \Ttest\geq\fhat_{\widehat\tau}(\Xtest)
   \middle|\Dtrain,\Dcal,\text{acquisition}
 \right]
 \geq1-\alpha-\Delta_{\rm ledger}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_coverage}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $P_\tau:=\mathbb P\{T<\fhat_\tau(X)\mid\Dtrain\}$ and
$S_\tau:=\sum_iY_{\tau i}$.  The first identity in
\eqref{eq:k2bc_lpb_cross_moment} gives
$\E[S_\tau]=NP_\tau$.  The second identity, followed by averaging over the
independent latent rows, gives
$\E[Y_{\tau i}Y_{\tau j}]=P_\tau^2$ for $i\ne j$ in Phase II.  Thus their
HT contributions are uncorrelated even though they are not independent.
Moreover,
$\E[\sum_{i\in\calItwo}Y_{\tau i}\mid\Dcalone,\Dtrain]=N_2P_\tau$;
hence the Phase-I and Phase-II sums are uncorrelated.  It follows from
\eqref{eq:k2bc_lpb_second_moment} that
\begin{equation}
 \Var(S_\tau)
 \leq N_1P_\tau(1-P_\tau)
      +N_2(\bar v-P_\tau^2)
 \leq N\bar v.
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_variance_bound}
\end{equation}
Cantelli's inequality therefore yields, for every $u>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P\!\left\{S_\tau/N\leq P_\tau-u\right\}
 \leq\frac{N\bar v}{N\bar v+N^2u^2}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_lpb_cantelli}
\end{equation}
At $u=\Delta_{\rm ledger}$ the right side equals $\delta$.  If the selected
bound has population miscoverage above $\alpha+u$, continuity and
monotonicity provide a deterministic boundary candidate $\tau_u$ with
$P_{\tau_u}=\alpha+u$.  Feasibility of the strict initial prefix then implies
$S_{\tau_u}/N\leq\alpha=P_{\tau_u}-u$, an event bounded by
\eqref{eq:k2bc_lpb_cantelli}.  Outside that event, selected miscoverage is at
most $\alpha+u$, which is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:k2bc_lpb_coverage}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:k2bc_shared_lpb} shows that row independence is not
fundamental: pairwise HT cross-moment preservation is enough.  Its
$O\{(N\delta)^{-1/2}\}$ gap is looser than the original Bernstein gap
$O\{\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N}\}$.  A deterministic maximum-weight bound would
permit a sharper martingale-Bernstein result.  The online controller does not
currently impose a useful uniform terminal-propensity floor, so
\eqref{eq:k2bc_lpb_second_moment} remains an explicit propensity-moment
condition, analogous to the original manuscript's bounded-mean-weight
condition.  Accordingly, the online method has an assumption-free budget
theorem and a valid LPB theorem under~\eqref{eq:k2bc_lpb_second_moment}; the
static all-high controller additionally preserves the original independent-
row proof architecture.

\subsubsection{Why the median, and whether another quantile is better}
\label{sec:k2bc_cutpoint}

The median is a stability choice, not a consequence of the DAPRO objective.
If $n_t$ fitting histories are active at time $t$, a $q$-quantile split
places approximately $n_tq$ and $n_t(1-q)$ observations in its two cells.
The median uniquely maximizes
\begin{equation}
    \min\{n_tq,n_t(1-q)\},
\end{equation}
and is therefore minimax for the smaller cell size.  This is valuable when
$N_1$ is small and the late-time risk sets are sparse.  It also makes the bin
assignments invariant to every strictly increasing recalibration of the
hazard score.  None of these properties makes the median variance-optimal or
budget-valid.

The distinction is transparent in a one-decision approximation.  For a
threshold $c$, let
\begin{equation}
 A_b(c)=\E[a(S)\mathbb{I}\{B_c(S)=b\}],\qquad
 D_b(c)=\E[d(S)\mathbb{I}\{B_c(S)=b\}],\quad b\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
Before clipping at one, minimizing $\sum_bA_b/p_b$ subject to
$\sum_bD_bp_b\leq C$ gives
\begin{equation}
 p_b\propto\sqrt{\frac{A_b(c)}{D_b(c)}},
 \qquad
 J^*(c)=\frac{
  \{\sqrt{A_0(c)D_0(c)}+\sqrt{A_1(c)D_1(c)}\}^2}{C}.
 \label{eq:k2bc_optimal_cut}
\end{equation}
Thus the population-optimal threshold minimizes the expression in braces,
not the occupancy imbalance.  If $r(s)=a(s)/d(s)$ is continuous and monotone,
an interior optimum obeys
\begin{equation}
 r(c)=\sqrt{r_0(c)r_1(c)},
 \qquad r_b(c)=A_b(c)/D_b(c),
\end{equation}
which need not be a median condition.

We tested one common quantile
$q\in\{0.1,0.2,\ldots,0.9\}$ at every time on four real setups, five
common calibration--test splits, both tasks, $N_1=50$, $B/N=20$, and
$\maxl=200$---360 fitted policies in total.  The response was the exact
fixed-target acquisition variance under the frozen no-CRC policy.  Relative
to the median:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Task & best stable fixed $q$ & mean variance change & wins/losses\\
\midrule
Metric estimation & $0.6$ & $-0.20\%$ & $11/9$\\
LPB fixed target & $0.7$ & $-1.65\%$ & $9/2$\; (six ties)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For metric estimation, the setup-wise optima were $0.5,0.8,0.3,$ and $0.7$;
there was no universal improvement.  For LPB, $q=0.9$ improved the mean by
$2.72\%$, but the gain was largely toxicity-driven, it lost on five
nondegenerate split/setup pairs, and its worst split was $4.19\%$ worse.
Changing $q$ did not change the selected LPB candidate or coverage on any of
the twenty outer splits; it changed only the small acquisition component.

Selecting $q$ on the same Phase-I sample by minimizing the fitted soft-prefix
objective was worse: the selected policy's exact variance was $0.65\%$ above
the median for metric estimation and $5.74\%$ above it for LPB.  The within-
split Spearman correlations between the fit proxy and deployment variance
were only $0.102$ and $-0.006$.  Searching the cutpoint enlarges the adaptive
policy class and introduces selection optimism.  At $N_1=50$, the median
should remain the metric default; a predeclared $q=0.7$ is a reasonable LPB
ablation.  An adaptively optimized cutpoint should use independent historical
data or a separate tuning fold and minimum-cell constraints.  Under either
Theorem~\ref{thm:k2bc_worst_path} or
Theorem~\ref{thm:k2bc_online_budget}, the cutpoint affects efficiency but not
the budget proof.

\subsubsection{Empirical comparison of the CRC-free controllers}
\label{sec:k2bc_experiments}

First, the existing production no-CRC matrix directly rejects the claim that
the one-turn projection reserve is a splitwise guarantee.  After de-duplicating
shared LPB rows by setup, method, and seed, the fractions of splits whose
conditional expected total cost did not exceed the configured budget were:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Task and budget & $N_1=50$ & $N_1=100$ & $N_1=200$\\
\midrule
LPB, $\bar B=5$  & $96.4\%$ & $99.6\%$ & $100.0\%$\\
LPB, $\bar B=10$ & $93.1\%$ & $95.6\%$ & $97.5\%$\\
LPB, $\bar B=20$ & $86.5\%$ & $92.0\%$ & $96.1\%$\\
Metric, $\bar B=10$ & $89.8\%$ & $96.2\%$ & $98.5\%$\\
Metric, $\bar B=20$ & $75.1\%$ & $82.2\%$ & $89.3\%$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At $\bar B=20$, maximum expected costs were approximately
$33.16,25.43,22.91$ for LPB and $28.21,24.23,22.82$ for metric estimation at
$N_1=50,100,200$.  The mean can remain below budget while individual split
policies transfer badly.  In a separate hallucination metric screen at
$B/N=20$, every tested quantile had mean expected cost between $23.29$ and
$23.57$ and maxima near $30$.

We next kept the soft-prefix target, hazard score, median K2 table, and DAPRO
optimizer fixed and changed only the budget controller.  The screening study
used four real setups, five matched splits, and $N_1=50$.  Budgets were $20$
for toxicity, Red-Team, and AutoIF and $10$ for hallucination.  The metric
results averaged over setups were:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Controller & expected cost & valid splits & variance & median max weight\\
\midrule
Online compensator & $17.24$ & $100\%$ & $7.02$ & $11.7$\\
K2 + independent CRC & $15.62$ & $85\%^{\dagger}$ & $10.05$ & $35.4$\\
Raw K2 + one-turn reserve & $17.11$ & $70\%$ & $10.39$ & $15.5$\\
All-high envelope & $7.94$ & $100\%$ & $33.37$ & $41.3$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here variance is the sample variance in squared percentage points across the
five random splits and is correspondingly noisy.  The daggered CRC column
reports the stronger per-realized-split population-cost diagnostic; CRC's
theorem is marginal over its independent control draw, so $85\%$ is not a
violation of that theorem.  The online controller's mean absolute log-change
to an active K2 probability was only $0.003$--$0.007$.  It was best on the
hallucination and AutoIF screens, while raw no-CRC was best on toxicity and
CRC was best on Red-Team; the small study does not establish universal
dominance.

For a sharper design check, we froze three toxicity and three red-team
policies and repeated acquisition fifty times.  The online metric variances
were $6.58$ and $8.16$ squared percentage points; the means of the unbiased
estimator~\eqref{eq:k2bc_online_variance} were $6.97$ and $10.18$.  The
agreement is consistent with fifty-draw Monte Carlo error.  The corresponding
within-split LPB coverage variances were $0$ and $0.058$ squared percentage
points.  Over the four five-partition LPB screens, the online controller used
$17.40$ expected queries on average and satisfied the budget on every split;
its selected-bound coverage variance matched raw K2 and the all-high
controller because their candidate selections were usually identical.

The static all-high theorem is exact and preserves independent rows, but it
used roughly half the available metric budget and suffered a large variance
penalty.  A per-row online ledger also preserves independence, but is worse:
if one requires $\sum_{t=1}^{M}p_{it}\leq b_i$ on every row path, AM--GM gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_i(M)\leq (b_i/M)^M.
\end{equation}
At $b_i=20,M=200$, this permits terminal probabilities of order $10^{-200}$.
The diagnostic indeed used only $1.9$--$2.4$ queries per metric row and
produced maximum weights in the hundreds or thousands.  Per-row pathwise
accounts should therefore be rejected.

\paragraph{Conclusion and recommended claim.}
The requested raw-K2 theorem does not exist: K2 removes the separate
projection regression but not adaptive empirical-to-population transfer, and
Propositions~\ref{prop:k2bc_counterexample}--
\ref{prop:k2bc_median_counterexample} rule out the desired conclusion under
the proposed premises.  There are instead four defensible statements:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Raw median K2:} efficient and design-valid after freezing,
    but budget control is assumption-based in finite samples.  It has the
    asymptotic guarantee~\eqref{eq:k2bc_gc_limit} for fixed horizon.
    \item \emph{Static all-high K2:} no CRC and a distribution-free,
    conditional expected-budget guarantee while preserving the current
    independent-row LPB proof; empirically too conservative for the main
    method.
    \item \emph{Online-compensator K2:} no CRC, exact conditional expected-
    budget control, near-complete budget utilization, valid fixed-target HT,
    and promising metric variance.  This is the recommended CRC-free metric
    method.  Theorem~\ref{thm:k2bc_shared_lpb} supplies an LPB extension under
    an explicit second-moment condition, with a looser confidence dependence
    than the independent-row theorem.
    \item \emph{K2 + independent CRC:} the clean current choice when the
    original LPB PAC proof and a finite-sample marginal budget theorem are both
    required without developing the shared-ledger martingale theory.
\end{enumerate}
Two bins make the online scalar correction particularly simple and make raw
transfer empirically more stable.  They do not make CRC mathematically
redundant.  The successful way to omit CRC is to control the predictable
expected spend directly, not to assume that the remaining projection error is
small.
